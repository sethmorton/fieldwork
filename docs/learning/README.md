# Learning science for US Vegetation

## Bottom line

The critique points in a useful direction. The dense-feedback concern is supported, while the species-only framing, interpretation of failure, and fixed study routine are only partly supported. Short, explanatory feedback and practice distinguishing lookalikes have good support outside GeoGuessr. The current card already includes scene cues and a truth-versus-guess comparison, so it is not simply a species lesson. Seth's reported 8% spotted over 24 rounds is a reason to change the teaching conditions, not proof that errors prevent learning. Keep the guess-first loop. Make each miss teach one contrast using one or two visible cues, then check a different unboxed scene. Treat the proposed session timings, cue count, and success targets as experiments, not scientific constants. The linked notes distinguish research findings from these Fieldwork inferences.

## Reading the evidence labels

- **Strong** means convergent experiments or a substantial synthesis supports the general claim. It does not mean the exact Fieldwork implementation was tested.
- **Moderate** means relevant experiments support the claim, with material, design, or generalization limits.
- **Limited** means a narrow study, expert comparison, model, or incomplete evidence supports only a restricted claim.
- **Inference** marks a proposed application, ranking, threshold, or mechanism not directly tested in this drill.
- **Local observation** means inspected code, prior local analysis, or Seth's report. It is not a randomized learning result.

A label applies to its paragraph or list item. Recommendations under an Inference heading inherit that label. Supported, Partly, and Not supported are verdicts on the critique, not substitutes for evidence-strength labels.

## How to use it

**Inference.** Change one teaching component at a time when possible. Compare equal practice time, keep test locations separate from training roads, and record delayed unboxed performance. Report counts and task difficulty alongside percentages. Easier practice can improve learning conditions without proving transfer. Higher scores on repeated images do not establish better region recognition.

Research citations use DOI, PubMed, or publisher links. Some publisher full texts require access. No cited study validates this complete US vegetation curriculum or an exact number of cues, minutes, or successful rounds.
