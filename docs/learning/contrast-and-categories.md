# Contrast and categories

## What must be learned

**Moderate.** Category learning requires recognizing what stays informative across different members and what separates one category from another. Carvalho and Goldstone experimentally varied visual category structure and study order. Interleaving helped generalization for highly similar categories, while blocked study helped some low-similarity categories. This supports matching practice to category structure, not always mixing everything. [Carvalho and Goldstone, 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24092426/).

**Inference and definition.** A characteristic feature is common in a region. A diagnostic feature changes the odds of that region relative to a particular rival. A pine can be characteristic of both Piedmont and Middle Atlantic Coastal Plain and offer little separation between them. Its relationship to terrain, ground, and companion vegetation may be more useful. Diagnosticity is relative to the alternative and viewing conditions. It is not equivalent to uniqueness.

**Local observation.** The current pack already combines plant, ground, road, and landform features. Its recurrence examples show that a feature appears in selected panoramas. They do not estimate how often it occurs throughout the region or how rarely it occurs elsewhere. The card's species caption distinguishes a species named in the panorama from a species merely common in the region. Preserve that distinction. See [vegcard.mjs](../../src/vegcard.mjs).

## When contrasting pairs beats separate descriptions

**Moderate.** Kang and Pashler tested learning artists' styles. Adding temporal gaps within blocked study did not reproduce the advantage of interleaving. Simultaneous cross-artist comparisons performed as well as interleaved study and better than massed study. Tests used new paintings. This supports discriminative contrast as a mechanism in that task. It does not isolate an optimum number of comparison images for landscapes. [Kang and Pashler, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.1801).

**Strong.** A meta-analysis of 59 studies found an overall interleaving benefit, with stronger results for visual materials and categories that were more similar to one another. Word-based tasks sometimes favored blocking. Similarity and material moderated the effect. Interleaving is not the same intervention as simultaneous comparison, even when both make differences easier to notice. [Brunmair and Richter, 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31556629/).

**Inference.** Prefer a targeted pair when Seth has a plausible rival and the two scenes can demonstrate a visible distinction. Give the pair a question, such as which scene has the wetter, flatter setting. Equal image scale and reasonably matched season reduce accidental shortcuts. Then return to a single-scene pin. Two-choice success has a 50% guessing baseline and cannot stand in for open-map region recognition.

**Inference.** Prefer a brief within-region sequence when the learner cannot recognize the common scene family at all, or when variation inside the region is the obstacle. Start with a clear example, then a different valid appearance. Soon mix its nearest rivals. A permanent long blocked tour is not required, and neither is immediate all-US scatter.

## A rule-writing format for this pack

All recommendations in this section are **Inference**. The examples reuse the current pack's descriptions. They are conditional coaching hypotheses, not validated ecological classifiers.

Write: **Both have X. Favor A when Y is visible. Favor B when Z is visible. If neither is visible, keep both plausible.** Select at most two discriminating cue dimensions. A dimension can be a contrast such as rolling versus flat terrain.

| Pair | Short rule to test | Main safeguard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Piedmont versus Middle Atlantic Coastal Plain | Both can have southern pines. Rolling terrain with exposed red clay favors Piedmont. Flat terrain with wet drainage favors Middle Atlantic Coastal Plain. | Terrain and ground condition are the two dimensions. Pine presence alone does not settle it. Wet ditches and clay are not exclusive. |
| High Plains versus Northern Glaciated Plains | Both can be open cropland. Dry shortgrass on a smooth plain favors High Plains. Undulating ground with visible seasonal wetland depressions favors Northern Glaciated Plains. | Use landform and moisture, not calendar greenness alone. Wetland absence in one frame is weak evidence. |
| Central Appalachians versus Northeastern Highlands | Both can be steep and forested. A dark conifer or a narrow forest road alone does not resolve this pair. Use a clearly visible regional scene relationship, or say that this frame has no reliable vegetation-only separator. | This is the only repeated named US pair in the prior analysis, with just two cases. Do not invent a species rule from that count. |

**Inference.** Author the rule for the current scene and the actual guessed rival. If the evidence cannot support a short separator, teach the broader family or inspect more scenes rather than force a confident answer. Keep a small collection of supported contrasts, not a permanent card for every historical singleton error.

## Perceptual learning is more than knowing the rule

**Moderate.** Kellman distinguishes discovery of useful features and relationships from fluency in extracting them quickly. PALMs use varied classification episodes with feedback and assess new examples. The medical review includes ECG learning, while the butterfly experiments tested adaptive category sequencing with immediate and one-week assessments. Neither tested US roadside recognition. [Kellman and Krasne, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1080/0142159X.2018.1484897). [Mettler and Kellman, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2013.12.009).

**Inference.** Writing a correct sentence is a useful check of understanding, not proof that Seth can see the cue at driving distance or apply it in a new season. Follow the sentence with an unboxed new location. Include counterexamples and cue-absent scenes so a tentative rule does not become a brittle shortcut.

## Measurable check

**Inference.** Track each direction of the confusion separately on fresh single-scene tests. Compare before and after training, keeping the same region mix and view constraints. Record correct pins, distance error, cue selection, false positives, and response time. Better paired-image matching without better unboxed pinning is incomplete transfer. Two errors are enough to choose a coaching example, not enough to establish a stable confusion rate.
