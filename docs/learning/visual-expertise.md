# Visual expertise: whole scenes and useful details

## Radiology

**Moderate.** A systematic review of 22 radiology eye-tracking studies found that expert performance was commonly associated with a global-focal pattern. Experts formed an overall impression and then inspected relevant detail. Task-specific patterns differed between image types. The included study of teaching search strategies did not find a significant perceptual-performance benefit. Expert behavior is not automatically an effective novice teaching method. [van der Gijp and colleagues, 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27436353/).

**Limited.** These expert comparisons cannot establish that teaching someone to glance quickly causes expertise. Experts already know which image structures matter. Nor does the review establish that local feature checking becomes unnecessary. Its global-focal account includes both stages. [van der Gijp and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27436353/).

**Inference.** Keep the full roadside scene available while teaching a crop. Let the learner notice terrain, canopy structure, openness, and ground together, then inspect the detail that distinguishes the plausible regions. Do not train only isolated leaves or prescribe an expert-looking eye-movement route.

## Birding

**Limited.** Hagen and colleagues compared bird experts and novices on same-species discrimination using full-view, central-view, and peripheral-view images. Experts' fastest responses were less impaired by peripheral-only viewing, without a group difference in measured eye movements. This suggests expertise includes better extraction of information, not merely a different scan path. It was an expert comparison with bird photographs, not an intervention establishing how to teach landscape gestalt. [Hagen and colleagues, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-37695-9).

**Moderate.** Bird-family learning experiments give a more direct instructional result. Testing improved classification of novel as well as studied birds, depending on study and test balance. Separate bird-family experiments found spacing benefits and examined simultaneous comparison. These support active, varied category learning rather than memorizing one named specimen. [Jacoby, Wahlheim, and Coane, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20804279/). [Wahlheim, Dunlosky, and Jacoby, 2011](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-010-0063-y).

**Inference.** Birding's combination of overall appearance and diagnostic details is a useful analogy. It does not establish that botanical naming or a field-guide checklist must precede region recognition. A feature that works on a close bird portrait may not be resolvable in a distant roadside tree line.

## Fingerprints

**Limited.** Thompson and Tangen manipulated noise, image orientation, comparison delay, and viewing duration. Experts outperformed novices in several matching conditions, especially for similar nonmatching prints. Their long-term recognition experiment did not show a general expert memory advantage. Their noisy matching task also found no inversion effect. Expertise is task-specific, and fast discrimination is not equivalent to photographic memory. [Thompson and Tangen, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0114759).

**Limited.** This supports a substantial non-analytic contribution to expert matching, but not the claim that all fingerprint expertise is holistic or that deliberate feature checking is useless. The experiment manipulated available information in experts, rather than randomly assigning novices to gestalt-only and feature-only curricula. [Thompson and Tangen](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0114759).

**Inference.** Include difficult nonmatches in vegetation training. If every dry, open scene shown during practice comes from the target region, the learner cannot discover which dry, open scenes belong elsewhere. Measure false positives as well as successful cue detection.

## Kellman and trainable perception

**Moderate.** Kellman and Krasne describe perceptual learning as improved extraction of relevant information. Discovery means finding useful structure, while fluency means extracting it efficiently. Their review covers medical PALMs and includes ECG training data with varied cases and feature feedback. This supports repeated classify-feedback episodes as a route to expertise. The combined training design does not isolate the causal contribution of each annotation or establish a landscape-specific optimum. [Kellman and Krasne, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1080/0142159X.2018.1484897).

**Inference.** A verbal rule can direct attention, but cannot substitute for practicing the visual distinction across seasons, viewpoints, land use, and locations. Gestalt and feature search need not compete. A short rule can teach what to look for, while repeated whole-scene decisions make that relation easier to recognize.

## What transfers

**Moderate.** Variability research finds that learning across varied examples can improve generalization, sometimes at a cost to early performance. That is not permission to mix arbitrary scenes or remove all initial guidance. Representativeness and the structure of variation matter. [Raviv, Lupyan, and Green, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2022.03.007).

**Limited.** Visual expertise is often specific to the practiced domain and task. Radiology, birding, and fingerprint results do not show that a generic observation skill will transfer to US region pinning. The PALM review explicitly discusses domain specificity. [Kellman and Krasne](https://doi.org/10.1080/0142159X.2018.1484897).

All recommendations below are **Inference**.

- Seek near transfer first: different roads within the learned scene families, with no boxes and the same viewing constraints used in play.
- Include ordinary, atypical, and cue-absent locations. A different crop of the same road is not a meaningful unseen test.
- Test both feature recognition and the geographic decision. Finding a pine correctly does not show that it narrowed the pin usefully.
- Treat transfer to a new season or region family as a separate outcome. Do not assume US vegetation training improves other countries.
- Use accuracy and distance as primary outcomes. Faster guesses are useful only when accuracy does not deteriorate.

## Measurable check

**Inference.** Reserve separate road corridors for training and assessment. Compare whole-scene plus targeted-crop teaching with the current card at equal total practice time. Test next-day and week-later unboxed pins, cue localization, and false-positive use. The success criterion is a better decision on a new scene, not a convincing explanation of a familiar one.
